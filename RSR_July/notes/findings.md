# Findings

Running notes from the replication and the two evaluations. Numbers are held-out
SimLex-999 Spearman unless stated otherwise.

## Barry's results reproduce

Two replicates, four modes, 8000 steps each, run from his code on our GPU with
the datasets regenerated by his own scripts.

| Mode | ours | his |
|---|---|---|
| baseline (step 0) | 0.570 | 0.557 |
| vanilla_all | 0.560 | 0.549 |
| vanilla_targetwords | 0.571 | 0.558 |
| rsr_all (templates) | 0.627 | 0.611 |
| rsr_targetwords (real sentences) | 0.477 | 0.486 |

Everything lands ~0.013 above his, which is almost certainly the corpus - our
Wikipedia stream collected 405k sentences against his 336k. Ordering and
conclusions are identical. His fMRI pipeline reproduced exactly too: 2,296
voxels, same as his report.

## Brain: RSA against Pereira, all 10 subjects

Our BERT checkpoints through his evaluation harness. RSA gain over the matched
MLM control, at layer 12 (where our RSR loss is applied):

| Partition | delta | t | p | subjects + |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All pairs | +0.048 | 2.93 | 0.017 | 8/10 |
| Both in RSR | -0.010 | -0.73 | 0.483 | 5/10 |
| One in RSR | +0.023 | 1.45 | 0.182 | 7/10 |
| Neither in RSR | +0.056 | 3.39 | 0.008 | 8/10 |

Against plain bert-base rather than the control, Neither is +0.047, p = 0.011.

Two caveats worth keeping in mind. Only layer 12 clears p < 0.05 - layers 9-11
point the same way but sit at p = 0.07-0.10. And the Both bucket is mildly
negative, i.e. RSR is slightly worse than the control on concepts it actually
trained on. That matches what we see on SimLex and is probably the
SimVerb/THINGS scales not agreeing with SimLex on those words.

For contrast, Barry's template RSR gets +0.007 on Both and -0.002 (p = 0.69) on
Neither. Opposite pattern to ours.

## The result holds without early stopping

Our numbers are early-stopped peaks; his are endpoints at step 8000. Not a fair
comparison, and it invites the obvious question, so: same recipe (weighted 0.7,
800 steps, 3 seeds), `--no-early-stop`, take whatever it lands on.

| Partition | mean | t | p | per seed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All pairs | +0.012 | 1.80 | 0.213 | -0.001, +0.021, +0.016 |
| Both in RSR | -0.019 | -1.39 | 0.299 | -0.046, -0.009, -0.002 |
| Neither in RSR | +0.044 | 5.68 | 0.030 | +0.035, +0.059, +0.037 |

So the generalisation effect survives (+0.044 vs +0.053 early-stopped, still
significant, positive every seed) but the all-pairs advantage doesn't. Fine -
the honest claim is the narrow one: RSR helps the words it never saw a rating
for, and that holds however you stop training.

Runs: `results/bert_continued_*_20260803_{004345,013634,022903}.csv`.

## GLUE: nothing broke

5 tasks, 3 models, 3 seeds. No collapsed runs.

| Task | vanilla | +MLM | RSR |
|---|---|---|---|
| MRPC | 0.865 | 0.855 | 0.854 |
| RTE | 0.668 | 0.653 | 0.659 |
| CoLA | 0.575 | 0.581 | 0.588 |
| STS-B | 0.893 | 0.896 | 0.888 |
| SST-2 | 0.928 | 0.927 | 0.926 |

RSR is inside the noise everywhere, biggest gap 0.013, and it happens to top the
three on CoLA - which is the syntax task, so that's the one where damage would
have shown up first.

Worth noting the small sets collapse very readily during fine-tuning. Without
warmup you get majority-class predictions and scores of 0.000 or exactly the
class balance. `eval_downstream_bert.py` uses 10% warmup, more epochs on the
small sets, three seeds, and flags any run predicting one class for 95%+ of
items.

## Loose ends

**Report vs code, in Barry's repo.** Three places the write-up and the code
disagree. None of them change his conclusions but they'd want fixing before
anything goes out:

- report says `rsr_targetwords` uses 4 edge-pairs per batch for VRAM reasons;
  `train.py:211` uses 24 for both RSR modes
- report says `rsr_targetwords` restricts target words to those overlapping
  SimLex; there's no such filter, the sampler gets the full `training_data` in
  both modes. `use_templates` is the only coded difference between the two.
- `eval_pereira_rsa.py`'s `load_or_simulate_brain_matrix` quietly swaps in a
  random matrix when the beta CSVs are missing. Since `data/` is gitignored,
  anyone cloning the repo hits that path immediately and gets numbers that look
  completely normal. Would raise instead.

**Still open.** Real-sentence RSR breaks in his setup and works in ours. Untested
differences: read-out layer (5 vs 12), learning rate (1e-5 vs 5e-5), and the
supervision pool.

**Also worth checking at some point.** He splits each word's 60 sentences 30
train / 30 eval; our BERT pipeline serves the same pool to both. Expected
overlap is only ~2 of the 20 eval sentences per word so it's probably immaterial,
and it can't touch the Neither bucket at all (those words are never RSR targets).
